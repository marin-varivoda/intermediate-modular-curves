\section{Points of degree 5 on \texorpdfstring{$X_0(N)$}{X0(N)}: proof of Theorem A}\label{sec:quintic-x0}

Let $C=X_0(N)$ for one of the thirty levels of Theorem~A, of genus $g$, and $J=\Jac C$. The rational cusp
$\infty$ identifies $\Pic^5(C)$ with $J$. For $r\ge0$ let $W^r_5\subseteq\Pic^5(C)$ be the locus of classes
with $h^0\ge r+1$. \Cref{tab:A} lists, for every level, the argument for each of the two assertions of
Theorem~A.

\subsection{The framework}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:inject}
If $C$ has no function of degree $5$ over $\Q$, then the points of degree $5$ inject into $\W(\Q)$, and a
point of degree $5$ is the unique effective divisor in its class.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $D$ is a point of degree $5$ and $h^0(D)\ge2$, a nonconstant $f\in L(D)$ over $\Q$ has a nonzero rational
pole divisor bounded by $D$; since $D$ is irreducible, it equals $D$, so $\deg f=5$.
\end{proof}

Up to isogeny, $J$ is a product of modular abelian varieties. Let $\A\subseteq J$ be the sum of the isotypic
components whose $L$-function vanishes at $s=1$; the vanishing is decided by exact modular-symbol
computations. By Kolyvagin and Logachev \cite{KolyvaginLogachev1989}, $(J/\A)(\Q)$ is finite. By Faltings
\cite{Faltings1991}, the Zariski closure of $\W(\Q)$ is a finite union of translates of abelian subvarieties;
a positive-dimensional component with dense rational points has its direction in $\A$. So it suffices to show,
in addition to excluding functions of degree $5$, that every positive-dimensional translate $T\subseteq\W$
with $T(\Q)$ dense has a fixed proper subdivisor on its locus with $h^0=1$: then only finitely many classes
can contain an irreducible divisor.

At each working prime $p\nmid N$ we check that the reduction of Magma's canonical model is an integral curve
of arithmetic genus $g$, with normalization of genus $g$ and a rational point (\S\ref{sec:prelim}), and we
enumerate all effective divisors of degree $5$ over $\Fp$, for every partition of $5$ into degrees of places.
If $x+B\subseteq\W$ over $\Q$, then the reduction contains the entire coset $\bar x+B(\Fp)$ in $\W(\Fp)$.

\begin{lemma}[Local rigidity]\label{lem:local}
Let $R=\Z_p$, let $Z$ be a closed subscheme of a scheme smooth over $R$, and let $U\to Z$ be a morphism from a
smooth $R$-scheme of relative dimension $n$. Let $u\mapsto z$ be rational points of the special fibres. If
$\dim T_zZ_{\Fp}=n$ and $T_uU_{\Fp}\to T_zZ_{\Fp}$ is an isomorphism, then $U\to Z$ induces an isomorphism of
completed local rings at $u$ and $z$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Lifting cotangent generators gives a surjection $R[[x_1,\dots,x_n]]\to\widehat{\mathcal O}_{Z,z}$; its
composite with $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{Z,z}\to\widehat{\mathcal O}_{U,u}\cong R[[t_1,\dots,t_n]]$ has invertible
linear part modulo $p$, so it is an isomorphism, and the surjection is injective.
\end{proof}
We apply this to $Z_S=\bigcap_{s\in S}(\W-s)$ for a finite subgroup $S\subseteq J(\Fp)$, after lifting $S$
to $J(\Z_p)$: prime-to-$p$ torsion points lift uniquely to torsion points over $\Z_p$ (and if $S$ lies in the
reduction of both a candidate direction and a known abelian subvariety $B$, the lifts lie in both); when the
direction is already known to be $B$, we may lift points of $B(\Fp)$ arbitrarily to $B(\Z_p)$, including
$p$-primary points, since both families are stable under all of $B$. At a class with $h^0(D)=1$ the annihilator
of $T_{[D]}\W$ in $H^0(K_C)$ is $H^0(K_C-D)$, and at a class with $h^0\ge2$ the tangent space is everything. A known family $V\subseteq\W$ defined over $\Q$
which fills the local branch then contains every candidate translate through that residue class. (For the
tangent space of $W^1_5$ and the base-point-free pencil trick used below see \cite[Chapter~IV]{ACGH1985}.)

\subsection{Excluding functions of degree 5}
\emph{The double-cover inequality.} If $C$ has an involution $w$ with quotient of genus $h$ and $g>2h+4$, a
morphism of degree $5$ cannot exist, even over $\overline\Q$, by \cref{lem:CS} (the degrees $2$ and $5$ are
coprime). This applies to $17$ levels (\cref{tab:A}). For $12$ of them, namely
$83,89,92,101,107,111,131,141,142,143,167,191$, these levels are already excluded in
\cite[Proposition~5.2]{DHJO} (for $142,143,167,191$ via a map of degree $4$ to $\PP^1$); it is new for $118,123,145,155,159$.

\emph{Published gonality.} $\gon_\Q X_0(82)=6$ \cite[Theorem~1.4]{NajmanOrlic2024}.

\emph{Empty special loci.} At $N=86,99,128$ and $p=7,5,3$ respectively, the numbers of effective divisors of
degree $5$ over $\Fp$ and of their classes are equal, so $W^1_5(\Fp)=\varnothing$, and \cref{lem:specialization}
applies.

\emph{Local pencils.} At $N=77,85,88$ the curve has $\Q$-gonality $4$ \cite[Theorem~1.2]{NajmanOrlic2024}. At
$p=5,7,3$, every special divisor of degree $5$ has $h^0=2$ and is $A_4+P$ for the unique class $A_4$ of degree $4$
with two sections, and $\dim T_{[D]}W^1_5=8-g+h^0(K-D-A_4)=1$ (the base-point-free pencil trick). The rational
tetragonal pencil reduces to a class of degree $4$ with two sections, which must be $A_4$ (an extra section would
give a class of degree $5$ with $h^0\ge3$ after adding a rational point); so it lifts $A_4$, the Abel map $P\mapsto A_4+P$ is an immersion, and \cref{lem:local} identifies
$W^1_5$ near every relevant point with this family. Its sections come from $A_4$, so a rational class in
$W^1_5$ has a base point. Level $74$ uses the same argument at the two primes $3$ and $5$. The possible positive-rank part $A$ is isogenous
to $E_{37}^2$, and the rank-zero quotient $R=J/A$ has $\#R(\Q)$ dividing $513$ (the gcd of its point counts at
$q\le31$). Since $\#E_{37}(\F_3)=7$ and $\#E_{37}(\F_5)=8$, $A(\F_3)$ is a $7$-group and $A(\F_5)$ a $2$-group, so
$A(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}$, which injects into both, is trivial. The four rational cusps generate a torsion group
$\mathcal C$ whose reductions modulo $3$ and $5$ have order $513$; it meets the torsion-free $A(\Q)$ trivially, so it
maps onto $R(\Q)$, and every rational class lies in $\mathcal C+A(\Q)$, whose reduction lies in
$\bar{\mathcal C}+A(\Fp)\subseteq\bar{\mathcal C}+J(\Fp)[\ell^\infty]$ with $\ell=7,2$. At
$p=3$ (using $J(\F_3)[7^\infty]$, of order $49$) and at $p=5$ (using $J(\F_5)[2^\infty]$, of order $256$) the
local pencil argument applies outside two exceptional cusp labels each, and the exceptional labels at the two
primes are disjoint; so every rational class is covered at one of the primes.

The remaining levels are treated with the finiteness argument below: rank-zero lifting at $96,98,100$ and the
quotient sieve at $79,103$.

\subsection{Route R: direct reduction}
\emph{Rank zero ($96,98,100$).} Here $L(J,1)\ne0$, so $J(\Q)$ is finite and reduction is injective on it at
the odd good primes $p=7,3,3$. This is the situation of \cite[Proposition~5.5]{NajmanVarivoda}. The computation
shows that every special class of degree $5$ over $\Fp$ has $h^0=2$ and is $\bar A_4+\bar P$ for a class $\bar
A_4$ of degree $4$ with two sections and a point $\bar P\in C(\Fp)$, and it lifts every such $\bar A_4$ to a
rational class $A_4$. At $96$ and $100$ every point of $C(\Fp)$ is the reduction of a rational point $P$, and
injectivity forces a rational special class to be $A_4+P$, which has the base point $P$. At $98$ only four of the
six points of $C(\F_3)$ (the cusps) lift. If $L$ is a rational special class, then $L-A_4$ is a rational class of
degree $1$ whose reduction is $\bar P$; its push-forward along the degeneracy map $X_0(98)\to X_0(49)=E$ is a point
of $E(\Q)\cong\Z/2$ reducing to the image of $\bar P$. For a noncuspidal $\bar P$ that image is a noncuspidal
point of $E(\F_3)\cong\Z/4$ (degeneracy maps send noncusps to noncusps), of order $4$, which is not the
reduction of a point of $E(\Q)$. So $\bar P$ is a cusp, and $L=A_4+P$ with a fixed rational cusp $P$.

\emph{Empty cosets ($85,88,92$).} Here $\A$ is simple, of dimension $2,1,1$. At $p=3$, the $\ell$-primary
subgroup of $\A(\F_3)$, for $\ell=17,7,7$, has no full coset inside $\W(\F_3)$; so no translate exists.

\subsection{Route Q: a rank-zero quotient (nine prime levels)}
For $N\in\{79,83,89,\allowbreak 101,103,107,\allowbreak 131,167,191\}$ let $Y=C/w_N$, $A=\pi^*\Jac Y$ and $R=J/A$; here $\A=A$ is simple
of multiplicity one and $R$ has rank $0$. With $t=[w_N(\infty)-\infty]$, the gcd $m$ of $\#R(\F_q)$ over
$q\le19$ odd equals the order of $\bar t$ in $R(\Fp)$ at the working prime, so $R(\Q)=\langle t\rangle$ is
cyclic of order $m$. Every rational class of degree $5$ lies on some $T_i=[5\infty]+it+A$, $0\le i<m$, and only the following
indices survive the coset test over $\Fp$: $i\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ for $N=79,89,101,131$; $\{1,2,3,4,23\}$ for $N=83$;
$\{2,3\}$ for $N=103,107,167,191$. For $g(Y)=1$ these are the families
$\pi^*\Pic^1(Y)+3P$, $\pi^*\Pic^2(Y)+P$, $\pi^*\Pic^2(Y)+Q$, $\pi^*\Pic^1(Y)+3Q$ with $P=\infty$,
$Q=w_N(\infty)$, and for $g(Y)=2$ only the middle two. The cusp is a fixed subdivisor: if $D$ contains no full
fibre of $\pi$ and $d+\deg D<g-2g(Y)+1$, then $H^0(C,\pi^*M(D))=H^0(Y,M)$ for every $M$ of degree $d$. At
$N=83$ the index $23$ is the family $\pi^*\Pic^1(Y)+D$ through an explicit $w_{83}$-fixed cubic point $D$: with
$F=\infty+w_{83}(\infty)$ and $c=[F+D-5\infty]$, invariance of $F+D$ under $w_{83}$, which acts as $-1$ on $R$,
gives $2c=5t$ in $R(\Q)\cong\Z/41$, so $c=23t$; the fixed subdivisor is $D$. At $79$ and $103$ the same sieve shows that all classes of $W^1_5(\Fp)$ lie over the indices $2,3$,
whose families have fixed cusps; so there is no function of degree $5$.

\subsection{Route T: known families (fifteen levels)}
For the remaining levels, $C$ has a double cover $C\to Y$ with $g(Y)=2$ or a map of degree $4$ to a curve of
genus $1$, giving a family $V=B+[F_4]+C\subseteq\W$ with $B$ the pulled-back Jacobian and $F_4$ a fibre (or
twice a fibre). For every tested subgroup $S$ and every full coset $x+S\subseteq\W(\Fp)$, the certificate checks (\cref{tab:routeT})
that $S\subseteq B(\Fp)$, that $x+S\subseteq B(\Fp)+[F_4]+C(\Fp)$, that $\dim T_xZ_S=\dim B+1$, and that the
differential of $B\times C\to\Pic^5(C)$ has rank $\dim B+1$. The subgroups tested cover every possible direction
in $\A$: for each prime order $\ell\ne p$ tested, every simple factor of $\A$ has Frobenius order divisible by
some tested $\ell$, and all subgroups of that order in $J(\Fp)$ are tested. At $82,99,128$ the unique positive
elliptic part is identified with $B$ through its newform, so the whole of $B(\Fp)$ may be used. At $86$ the
repeated elliptic factor is isogenous to $E_{43}\colon y^2+y=x^3+x^2$, which has no rational $2$-isogeny, so every
curve in its isogeny class is linked to it by an odd-degree isogeny and has cyclic group of order $8$ over $\F_7$;
all $192$ cyclic subgroups of order $8$ of $J(\F_7)$ are tested. By \cref{lem:local}, every translate $T$ lies in $V$.
Projecting to $J/B$, which has dimension $>1$, shows $T\subseteq B+[F_4]+P$ for a fixed point $P$, whose Galois
orbit is then a proper fixed subdivisor.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem A]
Combine the function exclusions with the three routes, as in \cref{tab:A}. The $36$ certificates are in the
accompanying repository (\texttt{certificates/A\_x0\_quintic}), with a list of the data per level.
\end{proof}
