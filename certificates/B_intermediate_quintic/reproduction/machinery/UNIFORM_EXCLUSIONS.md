# Uniform finiteness certificates

## Rank zero plus rational gonality greater than five

Fresh Magma central-value tests compute L(J1(N),1) exactly via modular
symbols. If nonzero, the modular nonvanishing theorem proves rank zero.
The natural finite map X1(N)->X_Delta(N) gives a surjection of rational
Jacobians up to isogeny, so J_Delta(N)(Q) also has rank zero. Nonzero
central value suffices; zero central value is never interpreted here as
a proved positive rank or as a failure of rank zero.

For strict curves of genus at least five, the published hyperelliptic and
trigonal classifications exclude gonality <=3. Theorems 1.3–1.5 of
Orlic, *Tetragonal intermediate modular curves*, give all remaining
rational gonality-four and gonality-five exceptions. These finite lists
are transcribed explicitly in published_gonality.py from the primary
source https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14512v1. No home-directory registry
is read. All subgroup generators are expanded by the new enumeration.

Outside those lists, gon_Q >=6, so no rational degree-five function exists.
Since a rational cusp identifies Pic^5(Q) with the finite group J(Q),
only finitely many degree-five classes are rational. An irreducible
rational effective divisor of degree five with two sections would yield
a nonconstant rational function with pole divisor exactly that divisor.
Thus each irreducible quintic divisor has h0=1 and is the unique effective
representative of its class. There are only finitely many such divisors.

This proves quintic finiteness with no full curve model and no finite-field
divisor enumeration. Each accepted ledger row has both an exact central
value transcript and the explicit theorem-list exclusion.

Important: a gonality-four curve is deliberately not covered, even if
its Jacobian has rank zero. It may still admit functions of degree five.

## Rational forgetting maps

If M|N and the image of Delta_N modulo M is contained in Delta_M,
the moduli operation (E,P) -> (E,(N/M)P) induces a nonconstant finite
Q-map X_Delta_N(N) -> X_Delta_M(M). It is well-defined on Delta-orbits
by the subgroup-image condition; equivalently the congruence groups
are included. When N=M it is the usual subgroup quotient map.

If the target has genus greater than one and finitely many quintic
points, it has finitely many points of degrees one and five. A quintic
point upstairs has image of degree one or five by the tower law.
Finite fibers then imply finitely many quintic points upstairs.

The inventory applies this rule to a fixed point and records the target
identifier in every propagated exclusion. It checks that no propagated
exclusion contradicts an accepted positive result. Negative dependency
chains start from previously certified negatives, so cannot be circular.

## Direct intermediate central values

Magma's standard JH(N,gens) constructs the intermediate Jacobian for the
subgroup generated by gens. We pass the full independently enumerated
subgroup, not just its index, and assert its dimension equals the genus
from our coset calculation. IsZeroAt(LSeries(J),1) then tests the exact
central value. Its nonvanishing proves rank zero of this actual Jacobian,
without requiring rank zero of the larger J1(N).

The rank-zero/gonality argument above applies unchanged. The ledger
records the specific transcript for every such exclusion. A vanishing
value supplies no negative or positive rank conclusion here.
