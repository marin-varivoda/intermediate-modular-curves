# Level 48: complete rational class sieves via pullback to X1(48)

The verifier is `run_case48_certificate.py`, run with Sage's Python and
either model key `2fea1fad025e6bab` or `efd0600330b90e51`. It writes a
classification certificate only after every step passes. The proof uses
`pullback_torsion48.py`, full Manin-symbol spaces and exact canonical models,
not characterwise integral lattices or a modular-unit database.

The X1 cusp classes are computed by projecting integral cusp-to-cusp
paths onto absolute rational homology along the Eisenstein subspace, modulo
absolute integral homology. Cyclotomic Galois sends a primitive cusp column
(x,y) to (a*x,y). The implementation checks permutations, group composition,
and compatibility of the action of -1 with the star involution on homology.
For weight two, the Eisenstein summand is the Hecke-equivariant complement
annihilated by integration against holomorphic differentials. Projection
therefore computes Abel--Jacobi classes, not an arbitrary splitting of the
cusp-boundary sequence. Absolute integral homology is checked as the
intersection of the full integral Manin lattice with the boundary kernel.

## The stronger restriction

[Derickx et al., Theorem 4.13](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.13929v2#S4.SS6)
proves that J1(48)(Q)_tors is generated by rational cusp-orbit divisors.
We compute these generators afresh in the full X1(48) Manin-symbol space.
No code from that paper or from the home directory is imported.

The forgetting map pi:X1(48)->X_Delta(48) has degree four. Pullback on
Jacobians is induced on absolute homology by transfer. We construct transfer
by summing transformed paths over representatives of Gamma_Delta/Gamma_1.
For a in Delta/{+-1}, choose d inverse to a modulo 48 and use the matrix

    [ a  (a*d-1)/48 ]
    [48       d     ].

It has determinant one and the required diagonal class. The separately
constructed pushforward simply regards each X1 Manin symbol as a symbol
for Gamma_Delta. The implementation checks integrality, preservation of
absolute homology, pushforward after transfer = 4, and compatibility with
complex conjugation and two good-prime Hecke operators. It also verifies
the transfer formula on every Manin generator, not just a selected basis.

Here is why this is the required map, independently of those checks. Away
from cusps, these torsion-free modular groups give an ordinary covering
of surfaces. Summing the lifts of a path over its four sheets is the
topological transfer. Cycles may be moved away from the finite branch set;
small boundary loops become sums of boundary loops upstairs and vanish
after compactification. Thus transfer descends to absolute integral
homology. Under the period description of Jacobians, this transfer is
pullback on Pic^0, whereas the ordinary pushforward is the norm map.
The forgetting morphism is defined over Q on point-marked models.
`RATIONAL_MODELS.md` explains why the canonical mu-models used for divisor
calculations are Q-isomorphic to those point-marked models; their rational
torsion orders are consequently the same.

If L1/Z^(74) is the computed rational cuspidal group of J1(48), every
rational torsion class x on the target satisfies transfer(x) in L1. Taking
this inverse image inside the target's Hecke torsion bound gives a new
upper bound. This argument does not require pullback to be injective.

The computed rational cuspidal group on X1(48) has invariant factors
[2,2,2,2,4,40,40,240,1436640], independently matching Table 2 of the cited
paper. Its order is 35,306,864,640,000. This is an additional check, not a
replacement for computing its embedding in homology.

lattices: x is retained precisely when its transferred vector lies in L1.

## Exhausting pencils and degree-five points

The independently certified canonical models recover rational closed cusps
as poles of a freshly Sturm-certified j-map. Their divisor differences
generate the lower-bound groups in the last column. Rank zero, independently
checked by nonvanishing of the central L-value, makes them torsion. Reduction
at 5 is injective on rational torsion (good reduction over the unramified
field Q_5). The matching lower and upper bounds prove that these subgroups
exhaust J(Q).

Each closed cusp ideal is reduced only after checking that its special
scheme is zero-dimensional of the same degree as its generic fiber. The
flat closure lies in that special scheme; equal lengths prove equality,
so the resulting reduction divisor is correct even for nonsplit cusps.
Enumerating all effective degree-five divisors over F_5, then retaining
special classes in the known rational subgroup, leaves four and 24 classes.
Each is lifted by a signed rational closed-cusp divisor. Semicontinuity of
h0 and injectivity of torsion reduction make this list exhaustive for
rational degree-five pencils.

For every retained class the verifier computes the full rational
Riemann--Roch space. If h0 is at most one there is no pencil; otherwise it
requires h0 exactly two and verifies that the ratio of basis sections has
degree less than five. Hence there is no base-point-free degree-five pencil.
As Pic^5(Q) is finite, an irreducible quintic divisor can then occur only in
a class with h0 at most one: a nonzero fixed divisor would contradict its
irreducibility. There are consequently only finitely many closed points
of degree five on each of these two curves.
