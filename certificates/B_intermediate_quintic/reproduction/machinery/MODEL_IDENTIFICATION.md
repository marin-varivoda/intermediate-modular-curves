# Identifying a canonical model by its Hilbert polynomial

Let C be our smooth modular curve, whose genus g has independently been
computed from its congruence subgroup. Our q-expansion basis is a basis of
the complete space H^0(C,K_C), not a selected subspace. The quadratic
product rank is 3g-3, different from the hyperelliptic value 2g-1. Thus C
is nonhyperelliptic and its canonical map is a closed embedding into
P^(g-1). Write Z for this image. It is a smooth geometrically integral
curve with Hilbert polynomial

    P_Z(t) = (2g-2)t + 1-g.

Let S be the projective scheme cut out by any collection of the exact
q-expansion relations. The vanishing bound certifies that every equation
vanishes identically on Z. Hence Z is a CLOSED SUBSCHEME of S, not merely
a set of sampled points in it.

Compute dim(S)=1, degree(S)=2g-2 and arithmetic genus(S)=g over Q. These
three checks say P_S=P_Z. The exact sequence

    0 -> I_Z/I_S -> O_S -> O_Z -> 0

then gives zero Hilbert polynomial for the coherent sheaf I_Z/I_S. A
nonzero coherent sheaf on projective space has a nonzero Hilbert polynomial
(equivalently, sufficiently large twists are generated by global sections
and have no higher cohomology). Thus I_Z/I_S=0 and S=Z as schemes.

In particular S is smooth and geometrically integral. No separate
smoothness or irreducibility calculation is needed. Equality of DEGREE
alone would not suffice: the constant term of the Hilbert polynomial is
essential to exclude embedded or isolated zero-dimensional contributions.

This gate does not assume Petri's theorem, or that quadrics suffice. If the
quadrics fail the Hilbert-polynomial test, more exact relations can be
added. It also does not manufacture a modular interpretation for arbitrary
equations: the full-basis, vanishing-bound, nonhyperellipticity, and rational
descent certificates remain required inputs. RATIONAL_MODELS.md establishes
the latter convention.

The older full smoothness/irreducibility checks remain independent
cross-checks on completed examples. This method applies to the same
canonical models and proves the same scheme-theoretic identification.
